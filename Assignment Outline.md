# WRITING ASSIGNMENT (OUTLINE)

**Title of the Essay:** Compare and/or contrast group study sessions with solo study sessions. 

**What is your Subject 1?:** Solo Study Sessions  

**What is your Subject 2?:** Group Study Sessions

## FIRST MAIN POINT (Difference)
**Main Idea #1:** Pacing
	
**Subject 1:** Solo Study Sessions 

**Major Detail A:** Students can choose the pace and speed for each topic, having full control over the speed they move through the study material.

**Minor Detail A:**
- Students can spend more time on concepts that are challenging to them, move quickly through material they already understand and skip over material that they are confident in. 
- This makes solo study potentially more time-efficient because this autonomy allows them to cater to their strengths and weaknesses.

**Major Detail B:** It is easier to implement personalized study techniques or methods that suit the student’s rhythm.

**Minor Detail B:**
- Students decide when to take breaks, how long to study, and when to shift focus, such as studying for 25 minutes and then taking a 5-minute break.
- Students could also adjust their schedule based on when they study with the most concentration, making it easier to manage if they prefer long periods of uninterrupted study.

**Subject 2:** Group Study Sessions

**Major Detail A:** It can be challenging to find a pace that works for everyone, especially if group members have different levels of understanding or interest.

**Minor Detail A:**
- It can be challenging to find a pace that works for everyone, especially if group members have different levels of understanding or interest.
- Everyone needs to be on the same page, so if one or more members struggle with a topic, the group may slow down to accommodate them, which could be beneficial for some but frustrating for others who grasp concepts quickly.

**Major Detail B:** The pacing might not be consistent throughout the study sessions.

**Minor Detail B:**
- A group might spend more time explaining and discussing topics, which can be beneficial for deeper understanding
- A group might have to switch topics frequently to keep everyone engaged, even if not everyone has mastered the current topic
- This can disrupt individual pacing and make it difficult for members who need more time to digest information

## SECOND MAIN POINT (Difference)
**Main Idea #2:** Skill Development
	
**Subject 1:** Solo Study Sessions 

**Major Detail A:** Solo study session can boost student’s self-discipline and improve their time management

**Minor Detail A:**
- Solo study session requires strategic planning and sustained effort.
-	Maintaining focus and motivation without the external accountability provided by friends or instructors is one of the significant challenges faced in a solo study session
-	This requires strong self-discipline and effective time management skills to create a structured study schedule and follow it to implementation

**Major Detail B:** Solo study sessions can improve students’ analytical skills and can help them solve complicated problems independently

**Minor Detail B:**
- Studying alone forces students to tackle problems and concepts without immediate outside help such as from friends or teachers
-	This encourages more in-depth thinking by the students, as they analyze the problem more sharply, searching and identifying patterns, and creating their solutions
-	It enhances the student's ability to evaluate their notes and make well-reasoned conclusions without relying on others’ interpretations

**Subject 2:** Group Study Sessions

**Major Detail A:** Studying in a group with others can help us to improve our skills by working together to solve problems while facing academic difficulties

**Minor Detail A:**
- Each member brings unique strengths and perspectives, which can decrease the time needed for a group to solve problems in their study
-	Challenges such as taking on leadership roles, presenting topics to friends, and engaging in constructive debates
-	It can help develop our communication skills, confidence, and the ability to articulate thoughts clearly

**Major Detail B:** Collaborative projects teach the importance of teamwork and accountability, preparing students for real-world scenarios where these skills are crucial

**Minor Detail B:**
- By navigating these challenges, students not only gain academic knowledge but also foster essential life skills that will serve them well beyond the classroom
-	Group study sessions also reduce wasted time and speed up the development of our skills as we are spending less time struggling and in confusion
-	Teamwork makes the dream work. Teamwork is key to success 

## THIRD MAIN POINT (Difference)
**Main Idea #3:** Resources
	
**Subject 1:** Solo Study Sessions 

**Major Detail A:** Without the immediate feedback available in group settings, students must find the resources by themselves while facing some problems and questions during their self-study moment

**Minor Detail A:**
- The lack of physical assistance can limit the depth of understanding, making it imperative to utilize a variety of resources
-	Examples such as online articles, YouTube videos, and interactive tools to gain a comprehensive knowledge base 
-	These challenges are difficult to understand and a waste of time when students need to know the entire method for overcoming the challenges
 
**Major Detail B:** With solo study sessions, students don’t have the privilege to get many different perspectives and resources

**Minor Detail B:**
- The lack of different perspectives and resources causes the students to be stuck with their solutions while ignoring other solutions that might be better for them
-	Students must spend more time finding explanations and resources that best explain and prove their solution
-	Students are less willing to search for different solutions or perspectives because these are unknown territories to the students

**Subject 2:** Group Study Sessions

**Major Detail A:** Groupmates have different perspectives and methods of note-taking, increasing the number of styles of notes available

**Minor Detail A:**
- Different students may interpret the same material differently, so when they compare notes within a group, they can gain new insights or knowledge that they might have not thought about
-	Someone may have highlighted key points other students might have missed, allowing each other notes to become more complete
-	This diversity in note-taking can enrich their understanding by allowing different perspectives to be discussed on the table 

**Major Detail B:** Having the ability to ask for different resources and solutions efficiently

**Minor Detail B:**
- Students can ask for resources and solutions to a topic they don’t have enough knowledge immediately in a group study session
-	Students can save time by not needing to search for answers that other students have already done their research on, saving time by not doing duplicating research
-	The efficiency of the study can improve as the student spends more time doing more questions and researching material that has not been explored yet
